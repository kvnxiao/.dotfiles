# Full-audit instruments

Load this reference only for a full audit or recurring ambiguity in a smaller pass. After reading the prose, run the mechanical tripwires. A match prompts a decision; it does not establish a defect by itself.

Each example below is written to transfer: no framework, package manager, language keyword, or repository-local name appears in any of them, and each repaired form is prose this skill passes unedited.

## Mechanical tripwires

- **Code paraphrase:** Flag any comment whose words restate the adjacent statement or call: `// parse the arguments`, `// lock stderr`, `// exit with 2`, `// increment the counter`. Delete it. Where the paraphrase exists because the statement is unreadable, rename or extract instead.
- **Test intent header:** Flag a comment above a test that names what the test function name already says. Delete it. Where the name is vague, rename the test and still delete the comment. Keep a skip or ignore precondition, a fixture requirement invisible in the body, or the regression the test exists to catch.
- **Linter over-compliance:** Flag a docstring that runs to multiple paragraphs on a private helper, or past one line on a public item whose signature already carries the contract. Compress to the single line the documentation lint requires, or delete it where no lint applies.
- **Negative or grievance rationale:** Flag a sentence explaining why a dependency's default is inadequate, why a feature is unsupported, or why an absent thing cannot appear. Delete it and keep the positive behavior. Retain external behavior only where the reader cannot act on the local rule without it.
- **Causal depth:** Count the causal links in one sentence: trigger, action, downstream dispatch, test or CI benefit. Past one link, cut to the immediate invariant. The downstream consumer documents itself.
- **Trailing modifier:** Verify that `, which ...`, `, where ...`, or comma plus `-ing` has an immediate subject and a clear logical relation. If its scope or causal force is ambiguous, split it.
- **Condition placement:** If a trailing temporal or conditional clause frames a long or multi-branch main clause, move it before the main clause. If a short trailing condition is restrictive or focal, preserve it.
- **Signature restatement:** Delete internal documentation that synonyms for the name, parameters, and return type reproduce without adding a constraint. Preserve documentation required for public discovery or ecosystem completeness.
- **Dummy verb:** Replace an incidental carrier verb paired with a nominalization, such as `performs validation`, `handles serialization`, `implements the transformation`, or `provides configuration`. If the noun names a concrete object, keep it.
- **Ambiguous reference:** If `from there`, `at that point`, `in that case`, or a bare `this` or `it` lacks an immediate unambiguous referent, name the file, buffer, component, or operation.
- **Artificial causal connector:** Flag a sentence opening with `That <verb>`, such as `That raises`, `That keeps`, or `That becomes`, and a trailing `, so ...` that carries a result. Both usually bridge a split the prose did not need. Rejoin the halves with parallel predicates, or give the second fact its own named subject. Keep a trailing `so` for an immediate mechanical consequence.
- **Demonstrative noun-echo:** Flag a sentence ending on a noun where the next sentence opens `That <same noun>`, such as `... without the schema file. That file is absent from fresh checkouts.` The echo re-announces a referent that the reader has not lost. Fold the second sentence's modifier into the noun's first appearance with a restrictive relative or a prepositional phrase. Keep the split where the second sentence carries an independent operational rule rather than a modifier.
- **Cleft:** Flag `X is what <verb>`, `what X does is`, and `it is X that <verb>`. The copula and the empty head delay the predicate, such as `The existence check is what keeps the spawn from failing` or `the rendered help text is what this asserts against`. Flag the locative and temporal heads on the same test, since `is where`, `is when`, and `is the reason` delay the predicate exactly as `is what` does: `the long help is where the flag appears` wants `the long help lists the flag`. Swapping one empty head for another is not a repair. Promote the actor to subject and let it act. Keep a cleft that contrasts one candidate against a named alternative already in the discourse: `The re-read, not the exit code, is what the launcher trusts` earns its cleft because both candidates are live and the sentence exists to choose between them. A cleft with no competing alternative on the page is delay, not contrast.
- **Reduced appositive:** Flag a trailing adjective or participial phrase attached to a noun with no relative pronoun and no finite verb, such as `without the binary artifact, absent from a stripped release build` or `reads the request file, unreadable after a key rotation`. The stranded phrase leaves the reader to infer which noun it modifies and whether it states a condition or a consequence. Restore the pronoun and a finite verb, or give the fact its own predicate. Keep the reduced form where the modifier is short and its subject is the immediately preceding noun with no competing candidate.
- **Coordinator chaining:** Flag repeated `and` or `or` that joins facts of unlike kind, especially where a participle was just removed. Test the members, not their number: `downloads, unpacks, and links the binary` coordinates three predicates of one kind over one subject and is well-formed at any length, whereas `needs no root and no network and can run unattended` welds two requirements to a capability. Recast a heterogeneous chain as a compound object, a parallel list, or a purpose infinitive; write `does not need root or network access to run unattended`. A serial list of like predicates is not a defect.
- **Placeholder referent:** Flag a clause whose only content points back at a prior noun, such as `X is one of them`, `The launcher polls for that file`, or `it does this for that`. State the fact about the named component instead.
- **Causal trailer:** Flag `, which keeps ...`, `, which lets ...`, and `, so <subject> resolves ...` where the trailer carries a purpose rather than an immediate mechanical result. These absorb a removed `That <verb>` bridge or participle and relocate the defect rather than fixing it. Front-load the purpose infinitive, or give the second fact its own subject. Keep the trailer for a result that follows mechanically and does not need a goal.
- **Over-front-loaded purpose:** Flag a sentence opening `To <verb> ...` whose purpose clause runs past roughly 10 to 12 words, coordinates two goals, or ends in a colon that splices the mechanism on. Count the words before the main subject: past that span the reader holds an unresolved goal through a predicate stack. Put the mechanism first and attach the purpose as a trailing infinitive or a second sentence. Keep a short front-loaded purpose that resolves in one clause.
- **Meta-documentation:** Flag prose whose subject is the documentation rather than the code: why a symbol is not a cross-reference link, why a fact sits in this comment, what syntax was chosen, what a caller `discovers`, `notices`, or `is told`. Delete it. Compression is not the repair here; a one-line `Not cross-referenced: ...` is the same defect shortened. Check whether a documentation lint or CI job already enforces the constraint, and delete on the spot where one does. Keep a note only where a maintainer could silently reintroduce a real build or runtime break nothing else catches, stated as the mechanical fact.
- **Textbook lecturing:** Flag a sentence whose subject is a build tool, compiler, runtime, shell, or operating system, stating how that tool behaves in general: `the build tool silently skips a target whose required features are not enabled`, `the shell wraps a thrown message in its own error rendering`. A comment documents this repository, not the tools it invokes. Replace the lecture with the local configuration rule and the artifact it governs. Keep the mechanism where the reader cannot act on the local rule without it and the behavior is genuinely non-obvious.
- **Staged contract:** Flag a docstring that dramatizes a problem before reaching the behavior: a default rendering described, then the requirement it fails, then `so this function ...`. Name each input branch and the action taken on it. Two or more clauses before the first verb describing the function's own work is the signal.
- **Over-fragmentation:** Flag consecutive short sentences that share a subject and were produced by splitting one clause. Recombine them unless each states an independent operational rule.
- **Bundled rules:** If operational rules joined by `and`, a semicolon, or a colon are independent, split them. If the clauses share a subject, mechanism, or consequence, keep the coordination.
- **Negation scope:** Verify a negative statement against its semantic scope and pragmatic context. If a positive equivalent is shorter and equally precise, prefer it. Preserve negative quantifiers and prohibitions that state the contract directly.
- **Actor-dropping passive:** Flag an agentless passive, or a quantifier or abstraction fronted as subject, inside a passage whose other sentences name one acting component: `Every outcome is written to the result file`, `The verdict gets recorded on each terminal path`. The construction reads as well-formed in isolation, which is why a reread of the sentence alone will not catch it; test it against the neighbouring sentences instead, and ask which component performs the verb. Restore the actor as subject. Keep the passive where the affected entity is the topic, where the actor is genuinely unknown, or where naming it adds nothing the reader can act on.
- **Displaced negation:** Flag an action verb left affirmative while `no`, `none`, or `neither` sits on its object or complement: `the spawn call hands the caller no process handle`, `the command writes no cached entries`, `the resolver reads neither lockfile`. The reader parses the action as happening, then cancels it on reaching the object, and a scan for `does not` misses the sentence entirely. Negate the verb: `does not return a process handle`, `does not write cached entries`. Keep the object-negative for a stative verb (`has no timeout`, `contains no timestamps`), formal schema or mathematical bounds (`accepts zero or more tokens`, `has no upper bound`), and a quantifier that states an API boundary over a set (`No caller can supply one`).
- **Dependency negation:** Flag `needs no` or `requires no` when `need` or `require` introduces a prerequisite, resource, or capability: rewrite `the installer needs no root or network access` as `the installer does not need root or network access`. Keep object-negative stative facts such as `has no timeout`, formal bounds, and API-boundary quantifiers such as `No caller can supply one`.
- **Resultative complement:** Flag an observation verb such as `shows`, `finds`, `reports`, `sees`, or `confirms` followed by a noun and a bare adjective: `shows the exclusions unchanged`, `finds the cache stale`, `reports the queue idle`. The adjective carries the entire finding and lands on a noun the sentence is not about, and a scan for negation misses it because the negative sits in a prefix (`un-`, `-less`) rather than on the verb. Restate the finding as a predicate: `shows that the write never took effect`. Reject the adjacent repairs on the same test, since `finds no change` moves the negation onto the object and `leaving the exclusions unmodified` keeps the resultative under a participle. Keep a causative subject that produces the state (`the rollback leaves the target absent`) and an `as`-marked status label (`reports the entry as unchanged`).
- **Contact clause:** Flag a relative clause with no `that` or `which` whose embedded subject is a full noun phrase, especially one hanging off a prepositional object: `writes every outcome to the result file the launching CLI polls`, `to keep the surface UAC must trust small`. The reader stacks nouns until the embedded verb resolves them, and any sentence left after the clause, a trailing adjective or a second predicate, compounds the stall. Restore the pronoun, or state the fact in its own predicate. Do not reduce the clause to an attributive participle: `the polled result file` drops the actor and trips the actor-dropping tripwire. Keep the contact clause where the embedded subject is a pronoun or one short noun and the clause ends the sentence.
- **Provenance:** Flag a clause naming where a rule, value, or line came from: a dated observation, a named audit or incident, a ticket or PR number, `we added this after ...`, `originally ...`, `historically ...`. Delete it and keep the constraint; version control already records the origin. Keep an external citation that settles a claim, and keep a date the reader must act on.
- **Tally or position:** Delete counts used only to introduce prose. Replace mutable-code references such as `the first`, `the second`, or `the remaining` with the item or shared property.
- **Fragment:** Complete a comment that opens with `Before`, `After`, `Once`, `Under`, `Without`, `Only`, or `To` but never reaches a main clause. If the following sentence carries the fact, a noun-phrase label may remain.
- **Narrator framing:** Delete intensifiers, indefinite agents, prose self-reference, staged reveals, and comprehension verbs such as `the reader knows`. Name the component, operation, field, or result instead.
- **Unsupported quantifier:** Verify every path covered by `all`, `every`, `never`, `always`, or a case sweep before keeping the quantifier.
- **Speculative benefit:** Flag a clause whose content is that a hypothetical later change will be cheap or that nothing downstream breaks: `so swapping the source later changes only the resolver`, `to keep a later cardinality change out of the table`, `so consumers still read one stream`, `no product requirement asks for it`. The syntax is not the defect, so a purpose infinitive is not the repair. Delete the clause, state the present rule with its actor, and record the future change in the roadmap or alternatives section where the artifact has one.
- **Negative universal as intent:** Flag `no other module reads`, `nothing else references`, or `only ever` used to state a design intent as an observed fact about the codebase. Restate it as a normative rule with its actor: `Every module must route table reads through the resolver`. Keep a negative universal the authority verifies, such as a lint that enforces it.
- **Antithesis slogan:** Flag a mirrored pair of clauses that contrast two components with a parallel verb and a swapped object: `the log records what changed; the ledger records what happened`. Replace it with the concrete distinction between the components. If they write records, name each record and its write condition.
- **Open-ended list tail:** Flag `and other X`, `and similar`, `and more`, `etc.`, and `such as ... and others`. Name the members or state the class the list exemplifies. If the source asserts unnamed members whose identities cannot be verified, flag the claim instead of narrowing its scope.
- **Undefined term:** Flag a compound noun used as a term of art, such as `the acquisition fence`, `an expand release`, or `a non-excluded column`, that no glossary row or earlier sentence in the document set defines. Search the corpus for the definition before ruling. Define it at first use, add the glossary row, or replace the term with the mechanism. A definition later in the same document is a forward reference, and the earlier site states the fact inline.
- **Qualifier stack:** Flag stacked qualifiers when they obscure whether conditions are conjunctive or alternative: `a credentialed owner for a non-deleted profile whose role does not require SSO`. Restate the conditions as clauses or cases that preserve the logic. Keep a compact noun phrase when its conditions remain clear.
- **Compliance verb:** Flag `obeys`, `respects`, `honors`, `follows`, `adheres to`, or `is subject to` before a mechanism name: `Acceptance obeys the per-account fence`. State the operation and any failure or effect that defines conformance.
- **Rule-standing adjective:** Flag `valid`, `proper`, `appropriate`, `applicable`, `correct`, `relevant`, or `existing` on a noun where the sentence never states the criterion: `preserves each valid existing relationship`. State the criterion or delete the adjective.
- **Absence as justification:** Flag a trailing relative or participle that justifies an action by asserting an absence: `drops the column, which no code writes`. Front the absence as the cause (`Because no code writes to the column, the migration drops it`), or delete the justification. Preserve the source's time scope. Do not coordinate the facts with `, and`, split them into clipped sentences, or repair the relative with a possession verb such as `held a value`.
- **Elliptical substitute:** Flag `none`, `one`, `the same`, or `such` standing for a noun introduced in an earlier sentence: `a workspace with none`, `a workspace that already has one keeps it`. The reader carries the antecedent across the boundary, and `with none` reads as possession of nothing. Repeat the noun: `a workspace that has no row`; `if the workspace already has a row, leave that row unchanged`.
- **Justification-plus-action coordination:** Flag a licensing fact and the action it licenses joined by `, and`, especially with an intensifier on the second subject: `no writer has ever stored a value in the column, and the same migration drops it`. Front the fact as the cause of the action: `Because no writer has ever stored a value in the column, the migration drops it`.
- **Possession verb on data state:** Flag `holds`, `held`, `keeps`, `kept`, or `retains` with a column, row, table, token, or person as subject and a value, row, profile, or state as object. For current state, use `is`, `has`, or `remains`. For a historical invariant, preserve the time scope with an action predicate. Keep the verb where a component literally acquires or releases something, such as a lock.
- **Additive paragraph:** Flag consecutive sentences when they share neither a relation to the paragraph topic nor a relation to one another. Parallel sentences may share one topic-level relation without connectives between them. Where no shared relation exists, state the missing relation or convert the run into a bulleted list or table. Run this check after the sentence-level pass.
- **Strawman contrast:** Flag a `, not Y` tail that denies a claim no reader would make: `the operator role is one such role, not the definition`. Delete the tail. Keep the contrast where `Y` is an inference the surrounding text invites, such as `team-member status derives from the user type, not from role rows` beside a roles table.

## Full-pass checks

All of these run inline, in the same session that composed the rewrite. Semantic advance and single-read clarity run during composition; bidirectional fidelity, deletion safety, the adversarial reread, and the idempotence check run once after it. The sections below are written for whichever agent runs them, since the user may still ask for an independent pass.

### Semantic advance

Require each sentence to add a fact, constraint, consequence, or necessary example. Delete a sentence that only paraphrases its predecessor. If a worked example makes an instruction executable, keep it.

### Single-read clarity

Read each prose item once with its surrounding code or document. Rewrite an ambiguous referent, condition, or scope. If the text is already clear, do not manufacture syntactic variation.

### Paragraph flow

Read each touched paragraph as one argument after the sentence-level edits. Identify how each sentence relates to the paragraph topic or an adjacent sentence. Parallel sentences may share one topic-level relation without connectives between them. Where sentences share neither relation and can be reordered without loss, state the missing relation or convert the run to a list. Where a new subject lacks a clear tie to the paragraph, state the tie. The one-link cap applies per sentence; the paragraph may carry a longer chain one link at a time.

### Bidirectional fidelity

Compare the result against both the original prose and the authority. Treat every claim as unverified until you open the authority and read it. A claim can be plausible, internally consistent, and false.

- Restore a verified invariant, number, term of art, boundary, or hazard that the rewrite dropped.
- Delete an assertion the rewrite introduced without support.
- Flag a contradiction between prose and implementation instead of preserving the old claim in smoother words.
- Follow a claim to the file that settles it, including files that the prose never names. A claim about a sanctioned lint carve-out is settled by the lint config, a claim about what CI runs by the workflow, a claim about what enables a build feature by the build recipe. A claim whose authority you could not locate is reported as unverified, never as accepted.

### Deletion safety

For each deleted fact, identify the authoritative site that still states it. If no site remains, restore the fact. When duplicate sites serve the same audience, keep the statement nearest the decision it governs.

### Adversarial reread

Run one final pass over items ruled clean, both the rewritten items and the ones kept unedited. Try to find a concrete semantic, convention, clarity, or diction defect. Read each rewritten item as prose you did not write: check it against the repair traps in `SKILL.md`, since a rewrite that trades a bare `this` for a trailing relative, or a participle for a coordinator chain, has relocated its defect rather than removed it. Read the rewritten items as a set as well; a recurring repair shape prompts a check for templated wording but may remain when every occurrence is independently clear. For a kept item, argue against its recorded keep-reason rather than restating the tripwire that reason already answers. If the pass finds a defect, correct it and reread the affected item once. Do not restart an unbounded file-wide loop.

### Idempotence check

Apply the audit criteria mentally to the finished result once more. Unexplained wording churn indicates that the criteria do not identify a concrete defect. Unless the new pass finds a named violation or a verified fact to add, keep the current text.

## Optional delegated verification

`SKILL.md` runs verification inline. Delegate only where the user asks for an independent pass, or where a wrong claim ships something irreversible.

Where a pass is delegated, supply the repository path and read tools, the candidate diff with both sides, the keep-reason ledger for unedited items, and this file with `references/diction.md`. Do not supply the drafting rationale, the candidate history, or your own assessment of the rewrite. Those are the anchors the delegation exists to remove.

The subagent runs bidirectional fidelity, deletion safety, the adversarial reread, and the idempotence check. It returns `ACCEPT`, or `REVISE` carrying, per item: the file and line, the named tripwire or refuted claim, the authority it read, and one minimal patch. Every patch names the rule behind it, and the subagent does not open a negotiation.

Apply each patch or reject it with a named reason, then report both. The exchange runs once. A second delegated round repeats the first at full cost.

## Full-audit record

Track each in-scope item as kept, rewritten, deleted, or flagged. A kept comment or docstring must have a named purpose: public discovery, an ecosystem requirement, a constraint, a hazard, required ordering, rationale, or another fact the adjacent code does not provide efficiently.

Report material changes and coverage. Unless the user requests a complete ledger, do not reproduce every clean sentence.
