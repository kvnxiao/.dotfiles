# Mandatory pre-delivery coverage pass

Perform this self-review after authoring or revising a solution and before delivering it. Inspect the actual content and rendered diagrams; schema validation and `covers` IDs are not evidence of technical coverage.

1. Map each in-scope prompt requirement to its canonical requirement, relevant interface/entity, and walkthrough step. Confirm an end-to-end baseline path for every functional requirement. Identify exclusions and assumptions explicitly. For consequential exclusions, record the baseline user experience and the requirement that would justify adding the capability.
2. Check that delivery order follows the prompt and that the opening Notes explain the progression. Require the relevant content phases, a baseline before any deep dives and a final summary. Check that omitted entity or interface sections are outside the prompt's scope and explained in the opening Notes. Check each reveal adds at most one section with a manageable group of related records. Keep main-board text as terse bullet fragments.
3. For each non-functional requirement, identify the mechanism and the step that explains it. Check operation-specific consistency/availability choices, partition behavior, authoritative state, durability, latency, and failure recovery. Do not impose mixed CAP choices when one choice actually fits all operations.
4. Trace representative read, write, retry and partial-failure paths across APIs, entities, diagrams and Notes. For each selected scenario, connect the user-visible outcome to the responsible state transitions, mechanism and remaining limitation; scattered topic mentions are insufficient. When lifecycles differ, check connection, operation, attempt and delivery ownership. For cancellable work, follow queued, executing and delivering abandonment through completion races and resource reclamation. Check identifiers, state transitions, idempotency, transaction boundaries, and acknowledgment claims agree. State external-provider capabilities as supplied constraints or explicit assumptions; remove guarantees inferred from domain-provider API research. Keep solution references focused on selected infrastructure building blocks.
5. Apply [architecture diagram rules](diagram-patterns.md): left-to-right primary flow, justified ingress, concrete products/modes, meaningful shapes/arrows, nearby annotations, and stable diagram evolution. Check that ingress fits the workload and explains routing, traffic distribution, and health checks where needed. Do not require a particular ingress label or product.
6. Compare the chosen design with applicable bundled patterns. For reservations, use [reservation patterns](reservation-patterns.md). Record include/defer/exclude decisions and triggers. Cross-check every scaling statement against the diagram: ingress, replica groups, state authority, and data-update paths must be shown or explicitly annotated.
7. Check the staff delivery standard: identify the problem's central tension, justify deep-dive priorities, commit to concrete choices, anticipate failures without waiting for interviewer prompts, and explain when a simpler or alternative design would be preferable. Record this evidence in the coverage review. Do not invent personal experience. Check deep dives explain limitation, change, alternative, tradeoff, failure, and diagram update. Explain only the mechanisms central to the question in detail; use familiar concept names elsewhere.
8. Build and inspect the output using the canvas acceptance checks when repository instructions permit. Check readability, incremental reveal, notes placement, camera stability, and offline packaging. Compare consecutive states: retain durable board content at stable coordinates, and require an explicit explanation for removals or replacements. Keep per-step talking points inside Notes. Record unverified interactions separately from successful structural checks.

Keep a concise `coverage-review.md` in the question package with requirement IDs, evidence step IDs, gaps or corrections, and verification results. Update it after content revisions. Do not mark a requirement covered merely because a step lists its ID. Fix substantive omissions before delivery; disclose unresolved limitations. Do not invent test results or guarantees to complete the review.
